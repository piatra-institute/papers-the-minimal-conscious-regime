# Research log

## Origin material (chats/, not committed)

A conversation of about 9,500 lines on what makes a living system conscious. It surveys criticality and neuronal avalanches, gamma and beta bursts, communication through coherence, travelling waves, thalamocortical loops and core and matrix cells, apical dendrites, mitochondria and anaesthetic action on respiration, astrocytes, respiration-locked activity, development (infants, metacognition), bodily self-consciousness and blindsight, and theories (global workspace, integrated information, higher-order, recurrent processing, biological naturalism). It then reorganizes the material into a "minimal conscious regime" with four constitutive invariants, a control layer and a separate space of self-related capacities, proposes ten predictions and a flagship within-subject anaesthesia experiment (MCR-1: propofol, ketamine, dexmedetomidine; discovery and confirmation cohorts; competing models A to G; six kill criteria). Its own critique says the invariants lack formal estimators, the non-redundancy of the four is asserted, and the statistics of a four-way conjunction are missing.

## What was kept, changed and dropped

- Kept: the four invariants and their names; the split into invariants, controllers and self-related capacities; the three-drug crossover with discovery and confirmation cohorts; the competing models and the six kill criteria.
- Changed: the invariants are defined by estimators on a fitted linear-Gaussian vector autoregression. "Stable multiscale organization" is operationalized as the test-retest stability of the slowest dynamically independent macro, scanned over macro dimension. "Temporal depth" is conditioned on the input path, so it counts only memory held by the system's own dynamics. The timing prediction is corrected: the conjunction is lost when its first invariant fails and regained when its last invariant recovers (the origin material required all four to fail before loss of consciousness, a stronger claim than necessity implies).
- Added: (1) a proof that R vanishes on region-acyclic operators; (2) the characterization of dynamically independent linear macros of a VAR(1) as invariant subspaces of the transposed operator, with a numerical transfer-entropy check; (3) the buffer example for T; (4) the dissociation atlas with its threshold interval and seed robustness; (5) the random ensemble and the controller sweeps; (6) the conjunctive-test statistics: margins with correlated errors, the PCI anchor, label noise, exact sample sizes with a design effect, McNemar power for necessity, order-statistic nulls for timing.
- Dropped: the conversation's French and Chinese recaps, its descriptions of its own reasoning, the long list of ten predictions (folded into the endpoints and kill criteria), speculative claims about artificial systems, and the personal framing.
- Not used: a 2024 Communications Biology paper on criticality under anaesthesia is attributed in the origin material to a different first author; the record (doi.org) gives Maschke et al., and it is cited as such. A Nature Reviews Neuroscience review that the origin material describes as a dendritic theory of consciousness (10.1038/s41583-024-00810-4) has a different title and scope in its record and is not cited. Several PMC-only references in the origin material (reviews on infant consciousness, thalamus, perineuronal nets, metastability) could not be resolved during this session because the PMC ID converter returned HTTP 429; the paper does not depend on them.

## Findings from the sources (tier T1 unless noted)

- [T1] Casali et al. 2013 (Crossref summary): a theory-derived index, the complexity of the cortical response to TMS, measures the level of consciousness across wakefulness, sleep, anaesthesia and brain injury.
- [T1] Casarotto et al. 2016 (abstract): benchmark of 150 healthy controls and communicative brain-injured subjects; an empirical cutoff discriminated conscious from unconscious conditions with 100% sensitivity and specificity; applied to 38 MCS and 43 VS patients it gave 94.7% sensitivity for MCS and found 9 of 43 VS patients with high PCI.
- [T1] Massimini et al. 2005 (abstract): in NREM sleep the initial TMS response was stronger but did not propagate beyond the stimulation site.
- [T1] Sarasso et al. 2015 (abstract): 18 volunteers; propofol gave a low-amplitude local slow wave with low complexity, xenon a high-amplitude global stereotyped slow wave with low complexity, ketamine a wakefulness-like complex pattern; no reported experience after propofol and xenon, long vivid dreams after ketamine.
- [T1] Radek et al. 2018 (abstract): interviews during target-controlled infusion of dexmedetomidine (n = 23) or propofol (n = 24); of 93 interviews, 84% included experiences from unresponsive periods (dexmedetomidine 90%, propofol 74%); 18 of 23 dexmedetomidine participants were arousable from the first loss of responsiveness, 10 of 24 propofol participants.
- [T1] Sikka et al. 2026 (abstract): scoping review of 157 studies (1921 to 2024, 87,866 participants); dream recall often 40% to 80% in experimental settings and 3% to 8% in large clinical trials; ketamine and propofol most consistently associated with recall; recall not associated with intraoperative awareness.
- [T1] Siclari et al. 2017 (abstract): awakenings from NREM and REM sleep with and without reported dream experience; experience associated with local decreases of low-frequency activity in a posterior hot zone, which predicted reports in real time.
- [T1] Maschke et al. 2024 (abstract): propofol, xenon and ketamine; unconsciousness characterized by distancing from avalanche criticality and the edge of chaos; resting-state dynamical properties predict individual PCI.
- [T1] Milinkovic et al. 2026 (abstract): dynamical independence on source-reconstructed EEG; propofol and xenon show more emergent but highly variable structure (fragmented macroscopic organization); ketamine shows reduced emergence with partial preservation of macroscopic structure; caution against equating emergence with level of consciousness.
- [T1] Breyton et al. 2025 (abstract): in a whole-brain model perturbational complexity appears only in a regime of high functional "fluidity"; resting-state metrics of that regime predict drug effects and agree with PCI in 15 subjects.
- [T1] Barnett and Seth 2023 (abstract): dynamical independence of macroscopic variables, a transformation-invariant information measure of dynamical dependence, explicit computation for linear systems.
- [T1] Barnett, Barrett and Seth 2009 (title and standard result): Granger causality and transfer entropy are equivalent for Gaussian variables.
- [T1] Suzuki and Larkum 2020 (abstract): three anaesthetics decouple apical dendrites of layer 5 pyramidal neurons; blocking metabotropic glutamate and cholinergic receptors or inactivating higher-order thalamus has the same effect.
- [T1] Redinbaugh et al. 2020 (abstract): thalamic and deep-layer neurons are most sensitive to changes of consciousness level across anaesthetics and sleep; consciousness depends on deep-layer feedback to superficial layers; stimulation of central lateral thalamus restored arousal and wake-like processing, location and frequency specific.
- [T1] Bastos et al. 2021 (abstract): propofol unconsciousness in macaques marked by about 1 Hz oscillations, Up/Down entrainment and decreased coherence above 4 Hz; thalamic stimulation reversed these features.
- [T1] Luppi et al. 2024 (abstract): under propofol, sevoflurane or ketamine, distributed activity becomes more constrained by structure; central thalamic stimulation reverses this; ventral lateral stimulation does not.
- [T1] Huang et al. 2024 (abstract): deep propofol sedation shifts thalamocortical functional geometry toward a transmodal-deficient pattern linked to matrix-cell composition.
- [T1] Whyte et al. 2024 (abstract): thalamic core and matrix roles for conscious state and content.
- [T1] Tagliazucchi et al. 2016 (abstract): propofol loss of consciousness reduces long-range temporal correlations in frontothalamic regions and decouples functional from anatomical connectivity, consistent with a departure from a phase transition.
- [T1] Hudetz, Liu and Pillay 2015 (abstract): in rats, the repertoire of intrinsic brain states falls at an anaesthetic propofol dose (20% fewer unique states).
- [T1] Priesemann et al. 2013 (abstract): avalanche distributions follow power laws in every vigilance state but differ by state; the brain operates slightly subcritical.
- [T1] Touboul and Destexhe 2017 (abstract): power-law statistics arise in networks away from criticality and do not establish it.
- [T1] Wilting and Priesemann 2019, O'Byrne and Jerbi 2022, Algom and Shriki 2026 (abstracts): controversy and the distance-to-criticality view; the ConCrit hypothesis that consciousness is lost as a capable system deviates from near-criticality.
- [T1] Lundqvist et al. 2016; Lundqvist et al. 2024; Fries 2015 (abstracts): bursty gamma and beta dynamics in working memory; beta bursts as functional inhibition; communication through coherence.
- [T1] Tort et al. 2025 (abstract): breathing-locked activity across brain areas, modulating faster oscillations and cross-area communication.
- [T1] Rodriguez, Peng and Lane 2025 (abstract): isoflurane and sevoflurane disrupt complex I-linked respiration in Drosophila and reverse the ATP synthase, at doses in the anaesthetic range.
- [T1] Zhao et al. 2024 (abstract): astrocytic Kir4.1 in paraventricular thalamus modulates recovery from sevoflurane.
- [T1] Lane 2026, Kleiner 2026 (abstracts): commentaries on Seth's biological naturalism; metabolic grounding; the need for an explicit definition.
- [T1] Bayne, Hohwy and Owen 2016; Birch, Schnell and Clayton 2020; Bayne et al. 2024 (abstracts): global states as multidimensional; consciousness profiles; C-tests and their validation.
- [T1] Bayne et al. 2023; Passos-Ferreira 2024 (abstracts): evidence favours early onset of infant consciousness.
- [T1] Gardier and Geurten 2024 (abstract): metacognitive monitoring above chance from about 3.5 years.
- [T1] Fleming 2026 (abstract): metacognition as structured transformations of uncertainty, integrated in prefrontal areas.
- [T1] Serino et al. 2013 (abstract): body ownership and self-location rely on distinct substrates (premotor cortex, temporo-parietal junction).
- [T1] Cogitate Consortium 2025 (abstract): preregistered adversarial test of IIT and GNWT (n = 256); results challenge key tenets of both.
- [T1] Nartallo-Kaluarachchi et al. 2026 (abstract): nonequilibrium and time-irreversibility as signatures of cognitive complexity and consciousness.
